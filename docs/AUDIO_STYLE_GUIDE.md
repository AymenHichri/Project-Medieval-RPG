# Audio Style Guide: The First Heresy

_Phase 7A Step 0a, audit Phase 4 (brief: `AUDIO_CATALOG.md` → "Audit brief"). Written 2026-11-17. Builds on `AUDIO_GUIDELINES.md` §1–4 and **extends** §1 with the brief's cosmic-horror and body-horror direction. Nothing here contradicts the guidelines' loudness, format or naming standards; where this guide is more specific, it wins._

> **In one sentence:** the world sounds **old, cold, low and close**: bowed strings and drones that seem to come from under the ground, a few human sounds (a lute, a voice, a bell), and now and then something that should not be there.

## 1. Musical identity

- **Register is identity.** Our strongest material (`Dark Fantasy Ambient Strings`, `Tracks wav`) lives almost entirely below 1 kHz (spectral centroid 120–200 Hz). Keep the score **low and dark**. Melody, when it appears, is a single line rising out of the drone, never a bright tune on top.
- **Stillness over momentum.** Exploration music has no beat, or a slow pulse at most. The beat is saved for battle, which makes battle feel like a rupture.
- **Three layers of the world**, each with its own sound:
  | Layer | Where | Sound |
  |---|---|---|
  | **The living** | Villages, roads, safe places | Medieval folk, sparse: lute, recorder, hurdy-gurdy, frame drum, solo voice. Sad, never jolly. The only warm timbres in the game |
  | **The dead** | Graveyards, crypts, ruins, battle | Low bowed strings, sub drones, tolling bells, ritual percussion (taiko, frame drum, bone rattles) in battle |
  | **The gods** | Shrines, divine encounters, story revelations | Distant choirs, dissonant clusters, intervals that "lean" wrong (tritones, quarter-tone detuning), sub swells, voices reversed or stretched. Used sparingly: rarity makes it frightening |
- **Silence is an instrument.** A map may have no music, only ambience. Story beats may drop the music entirely before a reveal.
- **Leitmotif.** One short motif (4–6 notes, minor/modal) for the First Heresy itself. It appears in the main theme, the menu, the boss music and the divine cues. Until it exists (commission candidate), `ForgottenSouls_Loop` carries the menu.

## 2. Instrumentation

| Use | Prefer | Avoid |
|---|---|---|
| Foundation | Low strings (cello, bass, viola da gamba), organ pedal, synth sub drones, bowed metal | Bright synth pads, EDM bass |
| Melody | Solo cello or viol, hurdy-gurdy, recorder, solo female or boy voice, nyckelharpa | Heroic horns, violin showpieces, electric guitar |
| Colour | Church and hand bells, harp harmonics, waterphone, bowed cymbal, dungeon-synth keys (dark, lo-fi) | Celesta "magic" sparkles, chimes in major keys |
| Rhythm (battle and boss only) | Taiko, frame drum, toms, anvils, chains, low choir stabs | Drum kits, trap/EDM hats, orchestral "trailer" hits as a style |
| Voices | Distant choir (ooh/ah, Latin-like nonsense), whispers, chant | Lyrics in a real language, pop vocals |

## 3. Sound design principles

1. **Physical and close.** Footsteps, blades and bones are recorded foley, not synthesised. Dry, short, a little room. They should feel heavy.
2. **Organic and wrong.** Horror comes from *real* sounds slightly twisted: a pitched-down crow, a breath reversed, a wet layer under a bone crack (body horror), a bell with its decay stretched. Keep it subtle; no jump-scare stingers.
3. **No cartoon.** No boings, comedic whooshes, "coin blips" or chiptune. Rewards are dull metal and leather, not sparkles.
4. **Variation is mandatory.** Frequent sounds have ≥ 3 variants or ≥ ±5 % pitch (`AUDIO_GUIDELINES.md` §4), implemented through `SfxSet.streams` / `pitch_range`, never by duplicating code.
5. **Data, not code.** Every sound is an `SfxSet` / `MusicTrack` / `AmbienceProfile` Resource generated by `tools/build_audio.gd`; tuning (volume, pitch, cooldown) lives in that tool's table.
6. **Mono in the world.** World SFX are mono, so `AudioStreamPlayer2D` can place them; music and ambience beds are stereo.

## 4. Ambient sound palette

| Place | Bed (loop, stereo, ≈ −24 LUFS) | One-shots (`AmbienceLayer.random_oneshots`, interval) | Positional |
|---|---|---|---|
| Village at dusk | Light wind, distant fields, faint distant voices | Dog bark (25–60 s), creaking sign (15–40 s), distant church bell (60–120 s) | Well water (later), torches |
| Graveyard / necropolis | Colder wind, a low drone of air | Crows, sparse (12–35 s); distant bell (45–90 s); a bone or chain creak (40–80 s) | **Bone pyre crackle** (`AudioStreamPlayer2D`, `Ambience` bus, max distance ≈ 600 px) |
| Crypt (later) | Room tone, drips | Stone settle, far-off moan (rare) | Braziers |
| Forest at night (later) | Leaves, insects | Owl, branch snap | Streams |
| Divine (later) | Sub swell, reversed choir pad | Whispers (rare) | The shrine itself |

Beds must loop seamlessly and contain no recognisable event that repeats (a bark inside the bed would repeat every loop). Recognisable events are one-shots.

## 5. Combat audio direction

- **Music:** battle music is the energy peak: driving low strings plus ritual percussion, 120–150 BPM, minor/modal, loop ≥ 60 s. Until a real track is sourced, `ForsakenStrings_Loop` stands in (≈109 BPM ostinato).
- **Hits land on the hit frame** (`BattlePresenter` already times FX on it); sound and FX share the frame.
- **Per-target hit sounds** tell the player what they hit: flesh (wolf, goblin) = cut + wet thud; bone (skeletons) = dry crack + rattle; armour (later) = metal.
- **Crit** = the normal hit plus a low thump and a slightly longer tail; **miss** = whoosh with nothing after; **defend** = shield brace. These override the per-target hit.
- **Monsters have a voice:** a cry on spawn and on phase change, a death sound. They identify the enemy even off-screen. The Charred Skeleton reuses the skeleton set with a lower pitch range.
- **Status ticks (Burn, Bleed) are silent**; the number popup carries them, so long fights don't become noise.
- **Magic:** a cast sound on the cast animation, an impact sound with the impact FX. Fire = ignite + roar; heal = low warm swell with a soft bell, never sparkly.

## 6. Divine and cosmic horror direction

- These cues are **rare**: a shrine, a revelation, a boss phase change, later a god.
- Techniques: choir clusters a semitone apart; extreme time-stretch of bells and voices; sub swells felt more than heard; sounds played backwards into a hit; a single whisper panned hard left or right in otherwise mono-centred material.
- Body horror: wet, organic layers (bone, sinew, breath) placed **under** the expected sound, never gore for its own sake.
- The divine is never loud; it is *close*. Mix it intimate, not epic.

## 7. UI sound direction

- **Quiet, short, low, tactile**: like leather, wood, paper and a muffled bell, not a phone.
- The Kenney CC0 set is used as the base. Pitching it 10–20 % down at runtime was not enough (playtest 2026-11-17: "odd for the theme"), so the copies are **darkened at import**: pitched to 55–80 %, double low-pass at 1.2–2.5 kHz, a short stone-room echo on confirm, open and rewards; focus uses Kenney's 10–30 ms micro-clicks (a dull knock, not a tone). `focus` ≤ 0.1 s; `confirm`, `cancel` ≤ 0.3 s; the typewriter `tick` is the quietest sound in the game.
- UI is on its own bus and is **never** stolen by world sounds (`SFXManager` rule). It sits in the 500 Hz–3 kHz range, which the score leaves almost empty, so it never needs to be loud.
- Long-term goal (Phase 12): replace the Kenney sounds with recorded foley in the same roles (page turn for open, leather tap for focus, small bell for confirm).

## 8. Mixing principles

| Layer | Target | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Music | **−18 LUFS integrated** (per file, before import) | Measured sources are −14 to −18 LUFS, so **all music gets −1 to −4 dB of gain** in the copy. Battle may sit +2 dB above exploration via `MusicTrack.volume_db` |
| Ambience beds | **−24 LUFS** | Exploration music sits *under or level with* ambience in quiet maps |
| SFX | Peaks ≤ −1 dBTP; balance by ear with `SfxSet.volume_db` | Kenney files are peak-normalised to −1 dBFS: start at −12 dB |
| Jingles | **−18 LUFS, mastered in the file** | `MusicManager.play_jingle` takes a bare `AudioStream` and plays it at 0 dB, so a jingle's level must be right in the file (or Phase 6 adds a volume parameter) |

- **Frequency lanes:** music owns the lows (below 500 Hz), SFX own the low-mids and mids, UI owns 500 Hz–3 kHz, one-shots (crows, bells) poke into the highs. Nothing should need to be loud to be heard.
- **Laptop speakers:** our music is sub-heavy (up to 62 % of its energy below 80 Hz, which small speakers cannot reproduce). The mix pass is checked on speakers as well as headphones, and a gentle low-shelf cut (−3 dB below 60 Hz) may be applied to the music copies if they feel empty on speakers.
- **Ducking** (existing): battle ducks ambience by 12 dB (`AmbientAudioManager.BATTLE_DUCK_DB`); a jingle fades the music out over 0.2 s (`JINGLE_DUCK_FADE`). Phase 7A adds an Ambience low-pass while the pause menu is open.
- **True peaks:** `6. Veil of Eternal Nightfall` (−0.7 dBTP) and the strings full versions (−2 dBTP) are fine after the −18 LUFS gain change; no limiter needed.

## 9. Music transition principles (with the existing `MusicManager`)

| Moment | Call | Timing | Rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| Map → map, different track | `SceneManager.change_map` → `stop()`, then the new map's `play(track)` | The old track fades out over its `fade_out` (2.5 s) while the screen fades; the new one fades in over its `fade_in` (4 s). Fades are linear in amplitude | Same track on both maps (e.g. village ↔ village interior) does **not** restart (`SceneManager.scene_music` reads the next map's track before loading it) |
| Map with no music | `play(null)` / `stop()` | 2 s fade | Let the ambience carry the space |
| Encounter | `push(battle, 0.4)` | 0.4 s, under the encounter flash | The exploration position is stored |
| Victory | `play_jingle(jng_victory)` | Music fades in 0.2 s; jingle 4–8 s | Starts when the results screen opens |
| Level-up on results | then `jng_levelup` | After the victory jingle (7A hook) | Short (1–2 s) so the results screen never waits on it |
| Back to the map | `pop(1.5)` | 1.5 s | Exploration resumes **where it left off** (`resume_on_return = true` on all exploration tracks); a still-playing jingle is stopped |
| Defeat | game over jingle, then silence or a drone | 6–10 s | No music on the Game Over screen after the jingle |
| Title | `play(mus_menu)` | 2 s fade in from silence | — |

**Requirements this puts on the files:**
- Exploration tracks must loop **seamlessly with no intro in the loop** (the `_Loop` edits do: intro 0.9–2.9 s, then `loop_offset`) and **must not have a big climax**, since a `pop()` can resume anywhere.
- Battle tracks should **start strong** (no long fade-in in the file), because the push is only 0.4 s.
- Jingles are non-looping, ≤ 10 s, with a natural ending (no hard cut).
- Ogg music: `loop = true` + `loop_offset` = the intro length. WAV ambience: loop points in the file (`smpl`) with *Loop Mode: Detect From WAV*.

## 10. Practical encoding decisions (for Phase 6)

- **Tool:** the Windows `ffmpeg.exe` bundled with ImageMagick (4.2.3) has `libvorbis` and the `ebur128` / `loudnorm` filters. It can measure, gain-adjust and encode the copies without new installs.
- **Music and ambience:** WAV sources → Ogg Vorbis q6, 44.1 kHz stereo (the 48 kHz `1. Whispers of the Abyss` is resampled), gain set to the targets above. The `smpl` loop start becomes `loop_offset` in the `.import` file. Because Ogg has no loop *end*, anything after the `smpl` loop end is trimmed in the copy.
- **SFX:** mono 16-bit WAV, 44.1 kHz. The Kenney Ogg files are decoded to WAV on import (tiny, instant start, one SFX format).
- **Originals** stay untouched in `assests/audio_sources/` (checksummed); `tools/audio_sources.csv` records zip → path → target ID → gain → trim.
